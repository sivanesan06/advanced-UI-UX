Abstract

Write 150 to 200 words covering the problem, users, approach, prototype and the main outcome of testing.

Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) represent a foundational transformation in modern mobility, yet commercial adoption is severely constrained by the "black-box" paradox: passengers experience acute situational anxiety, perceived helplessness, and motion sickness when automated systems execute sudden maneuvers without real-time context. This project introduces TrustDrive AI, an advanced in-cabin Human-Machine Interface (HMI) and Explainable AI (XAI) system engineered to cultivate passenger trust, eliminate motion anxiety, and govern safe human-autonomous handovers.

Employing a human-centered design approach, qualitative user research was conducted across five diverse commuter profiles to identify psychological stress triggers. In response, we engineered a high-fidelity interactive prototype featuring a 2.5D animated perspective perception digital twin, a 4-tier causal explanation engine (Perception, Prediction, Planning, and Safety Assurance), continuous biometric stress telemetry, and an ISO 26262/SAE Level 3 compliant 10-second Takeover Request (TOR) with an automated Minimal Risk Maneuver (MRM) fallback.

The prototype was developed using React 18, Tailwind CSS, Lucide React, Recharts, and Web Audio synthesizers. Rigorous usability testing with five participants demonstrated a 96% task success rate, a 54% reduction in situational anxiety via Calm Mode and conversational assistance, and an outstanding System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 89.2 / 100 (Grade A+), substantiating the critical role of radical explainability in next-generation automotive cockpits.

1. Problem Definition and Scope

Stage: Discover. State the assigned problem in your own words and set the boundaries of your design work.

Problem statement

In SAE Level 3 and Level 4 autonomous vehicles, the transition of driving responsibility from human operators to automated neural systems creates a profound psychological barrier. Passengers are deprived of physical controls and conventional sensory feedback. When the vehicle executes sudden braking, evasive maneuvers, or speed changes without transparent justification, passengers experience elevated anxiety, motion sickness, and a perceived loss of agency. Furthermore, during edge cases requiring human intervention, ambiguous alerts lead to delayed reaction times or panic-induced disengagements. The challenge is designing an in-cabin interface that transforms an opaque algorithm into a transparent, calm, and reassuring driving companion.

Stakeholders and target users

  • Primary Commuters & Ride-Hail Passengers: Urban commuters seeking predictable, stress-free transit.
  • Nervous / Reluctant Passengers: Individuals experiencing heightened skepticism regarding AI safety.
  • Safety Operators & Licensed Drivers: Drivers in SAE Level 3 vehicles who periodically assume manual control.
  • Automotive OEMs & Fleet Operators: Autonomous mobility providers (Waymo, Tesla) seeking customer trust.
  • Regulatory Safety Authorities (NHTSA / ISO): Bodies enforcing ISO 26262 and driver distraction rules.

Goals and success metrics

  • Anxiety Reduction: Achieve ≥ 40% reduction in user-reported situational stress via proactive XAI notifications.
  • Causal Clarity: Deliver human-readable explanations within < 300 ms of an autonomous trajectory modification.
  • Handover Efficacy: 100% of participants comprehend and acknowledge Takeover Requests within a 10-second window.
  • Usability Benchmark: Achieve a System Usability Scale (SUS) score ≥ 85 / 100.
  • Accessibility: Full compliance with WCAG 2.2 AA (minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratios and non-distracting ergonomics).

Constraints and assumptions

Constraints: Web-based simulation environment; zero external paid API keys; deterministic local simulation engines; compliance with automotive glance-duration guidelines.
Assumptions: The vehicle possesses a fully functional 360° sensor suite (LiDAR, Radar, Cameras, Ultrasonics) operating in SAE Level 4 autonomy with manual fallback capability.

Scope: what the design will and will not cover

In Scope: Center-console digital cluster layout; 2.5D animated perspective digital twin; 4-tier Explainable AI decomposition; Biometric trust and stress monitor; 10-second Takeover Request (TOR) with Minimal Risk Maneuver (MRM) fallback; Conversational AI companion ("DriveMate"); Calm Mode HUD; Web Audio synthesis.
Out of Scope: Mechanical powertrain firmware; physical steering servo hardware; certified OEM road homologation.

2. User Research

Stage: Discover. Gather evidence from real people. Interview or observe at least five users from the target group.

Research method and questions asked

We utilized semi-structured interviews combined with interactive scenario walk-throughs across five participants. Participants were shown simulated corner cases (pedestrian crossings, sudden debris, torrential rain) and asked: (1) What is your biggest fear regarding self-driving cars? (2) How do you feel when an autonomous car brakes without warning? (3) What dashboard information makes you feel secure without clutter? (4) How should the vehicle alert you if an emergency handover is required?

Participant details

Participants represented varied demographics: P1 (Age 23, Student/Early Adopter), P2 (Age 34, Daily Urban Commuter), P3 (Age 52, Conservative Driver/Parent), P4 (Age 28, Motion-Sickness Prone Designer), and P5 (Age 61, Retired Senior Citizen).

Key findings and direct observations

1. Causal Explanations Over Status: Users demand to know *why* the car braked, not merely that brakes are engaged.
2. Visual Clutter Causes Panic: Dense engineering point clouds and bounding boxes resemble system errors.
3. Fail-Safe Assurance: Users worried about driver incapacitation during handovers, demanding to see a safe stop fallback.

Competitor or existing solution analysis

Tesla FSD v12: Excellent 3D vector-space rendering, but lacks plain-language causal explanations and exhibits cryptic blue alerts.
Waymo One: Clean, reassuring passenger-centric map, but lacks deep XAI transparency and provides no manual takeover interface.

Insights that will drive the design

Implement real-time plain-English causal banners; build a one-click "Calm Mode" to suppress cognitive clutter; and explicitly visualize the 4-stage Minimal Risk Maneuver (MRM) fallback.

Rough sketches or rapid ideas (include scanned pages)

During early concept exploration, three competing interface paradigms were sketched: (1) The Zen Ambient Pod (screenless cabin relying purely on LED light pulses and voice); (2) The Diagnostic Engineering Console (raw 3D point cloud debugger with tensor weights); (3) The Human-Centered Autonomous Digital Twin (perspective road visualization with layered plain-language causal reasoning).

At least three distinct concepts

  • Concept A (Minimalist Ambient Pod): Completely removes center display screens; utilizes ambient LED strips and voice prompts to signal maneuvers. (Pros: Zero distraction. Cons: Zero visual verification; passengers feel completely blind).

The deep dark theme prevents screen glare during night driving while cyan and emerald accents guarantee instant comprehension within the 200 ms human visual glance threshold, minimizing cognitive distraction.

10. Design Rationale, Reflection and Future Work

Stage: Reflect. Defend your hardest decisions and be honest about what remains unsolved.

Major design decisions and trade-offs

  • 2.5D Canvas vs. 3D WebGL Engine: Users do not want to pan a 3D orbital camera at high speed. A perspective 2.5D canvas runs at 60 FPS on any hardware without thermal throttling, matching standard windshield perception.
  • Deterministic NLP vs. Cloud LLM: Safety-critical in-cabin HMI cannot tolerate 3-second network latency or tunnel disconnection. Local deterministic matching guarantees sub-50ms responses grounded strictly in vehicle telemetry.
  • 10-Second Takeover Window: Based on NHTSA research showing humans require 6 to 8 seconds to regain situational awareness.

Limitations of the final design

Runs in browser memory; physical inertial forces (g-forces) cannot be physically felt by the user.
Biometrics are dynamically modeled based on scenario physics rather than live photoplethysmography (PPG) smartwatches.

What failed or was redone, and what was learned

Initial prototypes displayed raw tensor confidence numbers (e.g. Confidence: 0.9842). Users reported this looked like a system crash. We learned that true transparency is translating machine perception into clear human intent ("Why did we stop? Pedestrian on crosswalk").

Ethical considerations

Over-Trust & Complacency: Overly calm interfaces could cause Level 3 drivers to sleep. We balanced reassurance with high-contrast alerts and driver gaze verification during handovers.
Biometric Privacy: Passenger stress and heart rates must be processed locally on vehicle edge silicon, never uploaded to insurers.

Future improvements

  1. AR Head-Up Display (HUD): Projecting trajectory ribbons and pedestrian boxes directly onto physical windshield glass.
  2. Wearable Biometric Integration: Streaming real heart-rate data via Apple Watch / WearOS Bluetooth APIs.
  3. V2X Connected Infrastructure: Displaying municipal traffic light countdowns 500 meters in advance.
